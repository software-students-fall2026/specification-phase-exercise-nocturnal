Rishi Ajoykumar conducted an open interview with Brooke – a graduate student and instructor of “Intensive Intermediate Italian”. Brooke was quite excited by the thought of live slide generation. She immediately asked for more details about document seeding and preparation of seeded content before lecture. Brooke even asked whether “exit ticket quizzes” could be generated multiple times throughout the class to check engagement and understanding (specifically mentioning zoom polls as a feature reference). Her concern before trying the tool was whether the focus on simplicity of slides would conflict with being able to show intricate information regarding grammar concepts and exceptions to rules on single slides. As Brooke tried a mock lecture on “The Slide Machine” her concerns were confirmed. She noted she’d either have to have seed images with more complex grammar tables and concepts in advance or live generation would need to do a better job of table layouts. Brooke also ran into issues with image selection, with the correct category of image being included but not the correct instance. Brooke extensively uses images in her lecture slides, oftentimes having three or four per slide to add context to unfamiliar words for students. The application was unable to support this workflow. Brooke was very satisfied with the quality of the exit ticket quiz generated. After using the tool, Brooke asked whether images could be previewed before being applied to a slide or what the workflow was in correcting slide mistakes during lecture. She explained that she felt correcting a slide verbally while lecturing through the slide machine voice command was jarring in that it disrupted lecture flow. Overall, she was impressed and felt the tool was better suited for domains that don’t benefit from more complex visualization and images. 

Goals / Needs:
- Seed projects with documents and prepare content before a lecture. 
- Presentation of intricate grammar concepts and exceptions clearly. 
- Tables and structure layouts for language instruction.
- Displaying three to four images together to provide context clues for students learning new language concepts. 
- Tools to check student engagement throughout class. 
- Issuing corrections to generated slide mistakes without interrupting lecture flow. 


Problems / Frustrations
- Current slide layouts do not appear to be capable of representing complex grammar subjects as live generation can’t produce sufficiently dense table layouts.
- Images can match the general correct category while being the wrong one overall.
- Application does not appear to support multiple images on a single slide
- Image previews were not apparent before an image was applied. 
- Verbally correcting a slide during a lecture feels jarring and isn’t always accurate.
