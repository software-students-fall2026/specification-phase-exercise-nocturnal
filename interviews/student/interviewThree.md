Daniel Zhang conducted a closed interview with Cathy. Cathy was first asked how she uses lecture slides. Notably, she mentions that she likes slides posted in advance as she previews them as a primer before lecture. During class, she follows along taking notes on additional details/context the professor adds to the slides. Later, she uses lecture slides to review. The most important aspects of lecture slides to Cathy were: formatting, being conscientious about information on the slide and limiting density of content per slide. She specifically noted a greater quantity of well separated slides were preferable to fewer and denser slides. Cathy’s first experience with “The Slide Machine” was mixed. She skipped the seeding phase by accident the first time, found slides advanced despite not wanting them to, and had issues with image selection. She was quite impressed with text cleanup (even with an at times unclear transcript) and stated the exit ticket quiz generation was “fair but not the questions I would have picked”.

Goals / Needs:
- Preview slides before class as a primer. 
- Follow along with a presentation during class.
- Record additional details and context provided by the professor. 
- Review slides later when preparing for tests. 
- See information separated across multiple readable slides. 
- Slides should be succinct without excessive content density. 

Problems / Frustrations:
- Accidentally skipped seeding phase during first-time use. 
- Slides advanced when she did not want them to. 
- Image selection did not consistently match the intended subject. 
- The generated quiz emphasized material differently than she would have liked. 
- Application’s behavior during live generation occasionally felt outside of her control.
