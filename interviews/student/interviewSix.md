Matthew Chen conducted a closed interview with Owen. Owen described his preferred method of using lecture slides, which included previewing them once before class, following along during lecture, and then reviewing them after if needed. He prefers to skim past what he already knows while noting anything important and rewriting the material in his own words. 

When preparing for a quiz, Owen heavily relies on slides to study. He uses them alongside practice problems and material provided by the professor alongside questions generated by an LLM such as ChatGPT or Claude. However, his initial preparation for a test is rooted in understanding the material on the slides first. When asked if he would tell a professor if he was lost in class, he said that he probably would not in a large lecture hall but might if he were in a smaller class. After testing out the application, Owen proposed a “lost” button for all students such that the professor could see how many students were lost at a given time during the lecture. 

Goals / Needs:
- Preview slides once or twice before lecture as a primer.
- Follow along with the slides during class.
- Review slides once after class when review is needed.
- Rewrite lecture material in his own words rather than reading passively.
- Skim past already-familiar content and record only what is important.
- Understand content from the slides before attempting practice questions.
- Signal confusion to the professor during class without drawing attention to himself.
- Annotate slides live, alongside the professor, from his own device.

Problems / Frustrations:
- Admitting he is lost feels harder in a small class than in a large one.
- He has no way to signal confusion during a lecture while it is happening.
